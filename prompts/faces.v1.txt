# Faces, version 1 (log entry 44): added to the system prompt, after the rules, when Video Scribe
# found the faces in a family video's pictures on the user's computer and tagged each person. The
# tags are a bridge: the describer keeps its own labels, and says which tag each label's person
# has, so that a name the family gave that person (in this video or an earlier one) can be put in
# on the computer. No name is sent. Kept apart from describe.v10.txt so that videos described
# before faces keep their descriptions (the checkpoint goes by the prompt's text).

FACES. Some pictures are followed by a line listing the faces found in them on the family's own
computer, such as "Faces: Person 1 (left), Person 3 (middle)." Each tag stands for one person, the
same all through this video: faces with the same tag are the same person, and faces with different
tags are almost always different people. Use the tags to keep your labels right: give each person
one label, by rule 13, and keep it wherever their tag appears, even in other clothes or another
place. A new tag in view is someone new, unless the picture plainly shows otherwise. A tag is not
a name: never write a tag in a description, as the listener never hears them. In "people", give
each person you labelled the tag of their face in "tag" ("Person 3"), or "" when their face was
not listed. The finder can miss a face that is turned away, small or blurred, so a person with no
tag in one picture may still be someone tagged in another.
