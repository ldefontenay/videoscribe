Video Scribe - how a described clip is scored (PLAN.md §3.4, spike 0(c))
=========================================================================

The bake-off (spike 0(h)) plays each described clip to the user, who listens without the picture.
The same clips, gaps and prompt version go to every service, so the scores compare services, not luck.


Part 1 - what the listener could tell (the user scores this, per clip)
----------------------------------------------------------------------

After listening to the clip once, with descriptions:

1. Who was there? Score 0 (no idea), 1 (some of them), 2 (yes).
2. Where was it? Score 0, 1 or 2.
3. What happened or changed? Score 0, 1 or 2.
4. Was any description wrong, as far as you know the video? Note which.
5. Did any description talk over someone speaking? Note which.

A clip scores out of 6 on questions 1 to 3; questions 4 and 5 are counted separately, because one wrong
name in a family video costs more than a vague description.


Part 2 - rule checks (Claude scores this from the text, per description)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each description is checked against the prompt's rules and the count of failures recorded:

- narrator words: "we see", "the camera", "in this scene", "the video shows"
- hedging: "appears", "seems", "possibly", "likely", "perhaps", "maybe"
- sound or speech: describes a sound, quotes or paraphrases speech, says someone talks
- over budget: more words than the gap's limit
- wrong moment: "seen_at" missing, outside its gap, or written as mm:ss
- name trouble: a name not in the known people (or heard and marked "heard"), taken from the file name,
  or given to two different people
- heard content: states as visible something only said on the soundtrack
- repetition: says again what an earlier description said
- not the requested language

Also recorded per service: money, wall time, requests, and how many gaps it left empty.


Part 3 - the number that decides
--------------------------------

For each corpus (YouTube, family): the average of Part 1 over its clips, then fewest wrong
descriptions, then fewest rule failures, then cost. The default service may differ between the two
corpora, and that is an acceptable answer.
